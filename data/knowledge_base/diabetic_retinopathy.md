title: Diabetic retinopathy - grading and referral
source: MedRAG sample corpus (educational summary)

Diabetic retinopathy is a microvascular complication of diabetes and a leading cause of visual loss in working-age adults.
Fundus signs of diabetic retinopathy include microaneurysms, dot-blot retinal haemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots and venous beading.
Hard exudates near the macula raise concern for diabetic macular oedema, which threatens central vision.
Retinal haemorrhages in all four quadrants, venous beading or intraretinal microvascular abnormalities indicate severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy.
Proliferative diabetic retinopathy with new vessels requires urgent referral to an ophthalmologist for consideration of laser photocoagulation or anti-VEGF therapy.
Patients with referable diabetic retinopathy or suspected macular oedema should be referred to an ophthalmology service and assessed with optical coherence tomography.
Good control of blood glucose, blood pressure and lipids reduces the risk of progression of diabetic retinopathy.
The differential diagnosis of retinal haemorrhages includes hypertensive retinopathy and retinal vein occlusion.
People with diabetes should have retinal screening at least every one to two years.
