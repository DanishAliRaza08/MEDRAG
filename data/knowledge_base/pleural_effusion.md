# Imaging features
A pleural effusion is an abnormal collection of fluid in the pleural space.
On an erect chest radiograph a pleural effusion causes homogeneous basal opacity with blunting of the costophrenic angle and a concave meniscus.
Approximately 200 millilitres of fluid are needed before blunting of the costophrenic angle is visible on a frontal radiograph.
# Investigation
Thoracic ultrasound is recommended to confirm a pleural effusion and to guide diagnostic pleural aspiration.
A unilateral pleural effusion should be investigated with diagnostic pleural aspiration, sending fluid for protein, lactate dehydrogenase, pH, microbiology and cytology.
Light's criteria are used to distinguish a transudate from an exudate.
# Causes and differential
Common causes of a transudative pleural effusion include heart failure, cirrhosis and nephrotic syndrome, while exudates suggest infection, malignancy or pulmonary embolism.
Bilateral pleural effusion with cardiomegaly is most often due to heart failure.
A parapneumonic pleural effusion with a pH below 7.2 indicates pleural infection and should be drained.
# Follow-up
Patients with an effusion of unknown cause after initial tests should be referred to a respiratory specialist.
