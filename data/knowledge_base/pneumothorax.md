id: kb-pneumothorax
title: Spontaneous pneumothorax
source: MedRAG sample corpus (educational summary)
modality: xray
---
A pneumothorax is air in the pleural space with partial or complete collapse of the lung.
On a chest radiograph a pneumothorax appears as a hyperlucent region devoid of lung markings with a visible visceral pleural line, often at the apex.
Breathlessness and pleuritic chest pain of sudden onset are typical presenting symptoms.
A tension pneumothorax is a medical emergency requiring immediate decompression and should be treated before radiographic confirmation if suspected clinically.
A small, minimally symptomatic primary spontaneous pneumothorax may be managed conservatively with observation and a repeat chest radiograph.
A large or symptomatic pneumothorax should be treated with needle aspiration or chest drain insertion.
Smoking cessation should be advised because smoking greatly increases the risk of recurrence.
The differential diagnosis of a hyperlucent hemithorax includes bullous emphysema, which can mimic a pneumothorax.
