title: Cardiomegaly and chronic heart failure
source: MedRAG sample corpus (educational summary)
modality: xray
---
Cardiomegaly on a frontal chest radiograph is suggested by a cardiothoracic ratio greater than 0.5 on a posteroanterior projection.
An anteroposterior projection magnifies the cardiac silhouette, so cardiomegaly should be interpreted with caution on such films.
Radiographic signs of heart failure include cardiomegaly, upper lobe venous diversion, interstitial oedema with Kerley B lines, and bilateral pleural effusions.
In a patient with cardiomegaly and suspected heart failure, measuring N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide is recommended to decide on the urgency of specialist assessment.
Transthoracic echocardiography should be arranged when cardiomegaly is found, to confirm heart failure and assess ventricular function.
The differential diagnosis of cardiomegaly on a radiograph includes pericardial effusion, cardiomyopathy and valvular heart disease.
Patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction should be offered guideline-directed medical therapy under specialist supervision.
