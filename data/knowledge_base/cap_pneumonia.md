# Diagnosis
Community-acquired pneumonia is an acute infection of the lung parenchyma acquired outside hospital.
Typical symptoms include cough, fever, pleuritic chest pain and breathlessness, with focal crackles on auscultation.
On a chest radiograph, pneumonia classically appears as lobar or patchy airspace consolidation, sometimes with air bronchograms.
A chest radiograph is recommended to confirm the diagnosis when pneumonia is suspected clinically.
# Severity assessment
Severity should be assessed with a validated score such as CURB-65, which counts confusion, raised urea, raised respiratory rate, low blood pressure and age 65 or over.
Patients with a CURB-65 score of 0 or 1 can usually be managed at home, while a score of 3 or more indicates severe pneumonia that should be managed in hospital.
# Management
Empirical antibiotic therapy should be started promptly, ideally within four hours of presentation, and chosen according to severity and local resistance patterns.
Pulse oximetry should be used and supplemental oxygen offered to maintain target saturations.
# Differential diagnosis
The differential diagnosis of consolidation includes pulmonary oedema, pulmonary infarction, aspiration, organising pneumonia and an obstructing tumour.
# Follow-up
A repeat chest radiograph about six weeks after treatment should be considered in patients with persistent symptoms or with risk factors for lung cancer, such as smokers and those over 50, to confirm resolution of consolidation.
